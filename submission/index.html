Material identification from Tanager VSWIR: mine-waste and alteration mineralogy blurred by broadband sampling

Alex Bradley · Department of Earth, Environmental, and Planetary Sciences, Washington University in St. Louis · abradley@wustl.edu

How much diagnostic alteration- and mine-waste mineral structure does Tanager's contiguous 426-band VSWIR preserve relative to Sentinel-2's broad bands, and what can that support as a screening product for acid-generating potential?

Impact statement

Tanager's contiguous visible-to-shortwave-infrared imaging preserves narrow diagnostic structure that broadband sampling blurs. This is library-anchored material screening: each mapped class traces to a measured mineral spectrum rather than a land-cover label. At two named US sites — Bingham Canyon and Goldfield — the maps show alteration-zone agreement with an independent USGS product, cross-sensor consistency with NASA's EMIT spectrometer, and a hedged acid-generating-potential layer for field follow-up. For state geological surveys and federal agencies (USGS, BLM, EPA) that characterize critical-mineral potential and mine-waste hazard, the deliverable is a reproducible candidate screening workflow — and a testable case for expanding the open Tanager archive over mining districts.

1Two named sites

The work targets two well-characterized US districts. Goldfield, Nevada is a classic acid-sulfate hydrothermal system next to the USGS Cuprite spectral benchmark. Bingham Canyon / Kennecott, Utah is a large open-pit copper-porphyry mine with extensive tailings ground and a clear need for mineral screening before field follow-up.

2The minerals leave a fingerprint

Mineral identity is anchored to the USGS spectral library (splib07a): every endmember is a measured library spectrum, nothing is synthesized. The diagnostic absorptions — the 2200 nm Al-OH doublet, the 2265 nm jarosite feature, the 2340 nm gypsum-carbonate feature, and the visible-to-near-infrared ferric-iron bands — appear in both the library spectra and the mean spectrum of the scene pixels the model maps as each mineral.

3What Sentinel-2 spectral sampling blurs

The case for contiguous VSWIR is sharpest at the 2200 nm Al-OH doublet, which separates alunite (advanced-argillic) from kaolinite (argillic) — a distinction that matters for both ore characterization and acid generation. Convolving the measured spectra with Sentinel-2's published response functions reduces their spectral-angle separability from 5.1° to 2.6°, a 50% loss, because one broad Sentinel-2 band (B12) spans the whole doublet. The loss is specific to the shortwave infrared: the VNIR jarosite–goethite contrast survives degradation, the control showing the effect is the doublet itself, not a universal multispectral failure.

4Similar in RGB, separable in SWIR

A deterministic difficult-case probe tiled the two lead scenes into non-overlapping 11×11-pixel patches, assigned weak labels from the corrected MTMF maps, and retained patches with at least 70% label purity. Of 268 retained patches, 415 cross-label pairs were unusually similar in RGB; 18 of those exceeded the 95th-percentile same-label SWIR spectral-angle threshold. The result tests whether the extra bands carry discriminating structure. Because the labels come from this pipeline, it is not an independent mineral-accuracy benchmark.

5Mapping the alteration

A mixture-tuned matched filter (MTMF) estimates per-mineral abundance against each scene's own background, with an infeasibility score that suppresses spectrally implausible detections. Composited into a dominant-mineral map, Goldfield shows scene-relative library matches that are consistent with parts of the published alteration context: a muscovite-rich core and an alunite-rich center and lineament at Cuprite. Kaolinite and iron-oxide agreement is weaker and is evaluated explicitly against the independent map below.

6Compared with an independent alteration map

At Goldfield the maps were compared with the USGS ASTER alteration map of the district (Rockwell & Bonham 2017), an independent product with no shared calibration. In the corrected descriptive pixelwise analysis, the Al-OH and gypsum-carbonate band depths each reach a rank-AUC of 0.78, while alunite and muscovite MTMF each reach 0.70. The Fe-oxide and kaolinite layers separate less cleanly — the published categorical classes group these phases differently than the spectral library does — and that disagreement is reported, not tuned away. Spatially blocked estimates are reported separately rather than treating adjacent pixels as independent replicates.

7Cross-checked with EMIT

Running the same library-driven pipeline on a pinned overlapping NASA EMIT scene gives a cross-sensor consistency check, not independent mineral truth, because the code and endmembers are shared. Scene-mean reflectance correlates at r = 0.962 over 240 shared bands (spectral angle 3.72°), and all six target-mineral maps correlate positively, ranging from muscovite (r = +0.335) to jarosite (+0.584). The map agreement is moderate, as expected from the date offset and different delivered grids. In these products, Tanager's 30 m ortho pixels cover about one-quarter the area of EMIT's ~60 m pixels; this is not a native-footprint claim.

8Acid-generating-potential screening

The secondary minerals that record acid generation are spectrally distinct. Jarosite is stable only in acidic, oxidizing, sulfate-rich conditions and is the diagnostic active-acid indicator; the iron oxyhydroxides are the higher-pH oxidation products; gypsum, absent the acidic iron phases, points to a buffered setting (Swayze et al. 2000). Each pixel is assigned an ordinal acid-generating-potential tier from the most acidic indicator present. At Bingham the high-potential pixels cluster around the pit and tailings ground.

Operational pathway

Tanager scene → authoritative QA and atmospheric masking → measured-library mineral scores → scene-relative candidate zones → ranked field targets → field spectroscopy and geochemical confirmation. The map prioritizes where to investigate; it does not replace sampling.

9Limits

  • The products have 30 m pixels; isolated sub-pixel features and fine mine-waste structures cannot be resolved.
  • Surface mineralogy only — not bulk chemistry, not depth.
  • Validation is against another remote-sensing product, bounding agreement at the alteration-group level, not per-mineral abundance; no field validation.
  • The acid-generating-potential layer is a relative, within-scene spectral indicator, and is unvalidated at Bingham, where jarosite support in the regional reference overlap is too sparse for inference.

10Reproduce & open tooling

The whole pipeline is one installed command over Tanager's Open STAC imagery:

uv run tanager-minmap hero --site goldfield --output out/

The repository contains the seven-stage tanager-minmap CLI and a declared reproduction path (see REPRODUCIBILITY.md). Public dependency resolution, clean-clone verification, and the citable derivative-map archive are release gates still to be completed.
